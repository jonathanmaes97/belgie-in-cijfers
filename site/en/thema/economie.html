State of the Union / Economy

Is our economy growing?

The economy is everything we produce and earn together. When it grows, there is usually more work and more money for wages and public services. Here you can see how fast the Belgian economy is growing, how much we produce per person and how fast prices are rising.

Who decides?

The European Central Bank sets interest rates in the euro area. In Belgium, economic policy is shared between the federal government (for example taxes) and the regions (for example support for businesses and investment).

Forecasts of the Federal Planning Bureau

How much more expensive will life get, and when is the next indexation?

3.7%

Inflation 2026, expected annual average

  • The pivot index is expected to be exceeded in November 2026 (Planning Bureau); then April 2027.
  • Inflation 2027: 3.7% (expected annual average).

When the pivot index is exceeded, social benefits and civil servants' salaries rise by 2%. Private-sector wages are indexed according to the rules of each sector. The smoothed health index decides when this happens: it excludes tobacco, alcohol and fuel, and is averaged over four months.

Economic growth

How much does the economy grow each year?

1.0%

Belgium, 2025 provisional

  • That is lower than the EU average (1.4%).
  • In 2015 it was 1.5%.

A positive figure means that together we produced more than the year before. A negative figure, as in the COVID year 2020, means the economy shrank.

Belgium, 2025 provisional

A measure of a country's prosperity. It is not an income everyone receives, but total production divided by the number of inhabitants.

Inflation

How much more expensive is life getting?

3.0%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (2.5%).
  • In 2015 it was 0.6%.

With inflation of 3%, what cost 100 euros last year now costs 103 euros on average. In Belgium, many wages and benefits rise automatically with prices (indexation).

Difficult words explained

GDP
Gross domestic product: the value of everything a country produces in a year. It is the usual measure of the size of an economy.
Real growth
Growth with the effect of price rises taken out. This shows whether we really produce more, not just at higher prices.
Inflation
How much the prices of what households buy rise on average in a year.
