State of the Union / Jobs

How many people are working?

How many people have a job helps determine how much tax and contributions come in to pay for pensions and healthcare, among other things. Here you can see how many adults work, how many people are looking for work and for how long, how many older people are still working and how many people are long-term sick.

Who decides?

Labour law and unemployment benefits are federal. Supporting job seekers is a task for the regions and the German-speaking Community (VDAB, Le Forem, Actiris and ADG).

A government is never the only cause of how a figure develops. Many figures only respond to policy years later, and they also depend on the world economy, European rules and the policies of the regions and communities.

Employment rate

How many adults have a job?

72.8%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is lower than the EU average (76.1%).
  • In 2015 it was 67.2%.

Students, retirees under 65, the long-term sick and job seekers do not work and lower this figure. Belgium aims for 80% by 2030.

Unemployment

How many people are looking for work?

6.2%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (6.0%).
  • In 2015 it was 8.7%.

According to the international definition: people without work who are actively looking and can start soon. This is not the same as the number of people receiving unemployment benefit.

Long-term unemployment

How many job seekers have been looking for more than a year?

31.6%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (31.5%).
  • In 2015 it was 46.2%.

The longer someone looks for work, the harder it usually becomes to get back into a job.

This figure plays a role in the debate about pensions and working longer: the more older people work, the more contributions come in.

Long-term sick (invalidity)

How many employees and unemployed people are long-term sick?

537,728

Belgium, 2025 provisional

  • In 2015 it was 346,971.

This is a number of people, not a percentage. According to the RIZIV/INAMI, mental disorders and diseases of the muscles and joints are the largest groups. The latest years are provisional: the RIZIV/INAMI may still revise them.

Definition and source
Definition
Number of employees and unemployed people in invalidity on 31 December: they have been unable to work for more than a year and receive a benefit from their health insurance fund. Self-employed people and civil servants with their own scheme are not included.

Difficult words explained

Employment rate
The share of people aged 20 to 64 who work. Part-time workers count too.
Unemployment rate
The share of unemployed people among those who work or are actively looking for work. People who are not looking, such as students, do not count.
