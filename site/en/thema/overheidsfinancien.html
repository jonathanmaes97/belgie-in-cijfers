State of the Union / Public finances

What state are the public finances in?

The government collects taxes and social contributions and spends that money on pensions, healthcare, education and roads, among other things. If it spends more than it receives, there is a deficit and the debt rises. The government pays interest on that debt.

Who decides?

Every government has its own budget: the federal government, the communities, the regions and the local authorities. The figures here add them all together, as Europe requires.

Taxes by economic function

Where do our taxes come from?

Of every 100 euros in taxes and social contributions that all Belgian governments together received in 2024, this is how much came from each kind of income or spending. Together that is €22,359 per inhabitant.

Taxes and social contributions are not all of the government's revenue: it also sells services and receives, for example, interest and dividends. All revenue together came to €26,068 per inhabitant in 2024, all spending to €28,357. The difference, €2,290 per inhabitant, is the deficit: the government had to borrow it.

Government debt

How large is government debt compared with the economy?

107.9%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (81.7%).
  • In 2015 it was 105.7%.

At 100%, the debt is as large as everything the country produces in a year. European rules ask for debt of at most 60%.

Tax burden on a lower wage

How much of what an employer pays goes to taxes and contributions?

41.4%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (39.5%).
  • In 2015 it was 49.4%.

Suppose an employer spends 100 euros on your wage. This figure shows how much of it goes to the government and social security, including your own income tax. You receive the rest as take-home pay. It concerns an example person: single, no children, with a wage of two thirds of the average. For higher wages the burden is higher, for the lowest wages it is lower.

Definition and source
Definition
Tax wedge: personal income tax plus employee and employer social contributions, as a percentage of total labour costs. For a single person without children earning 67% of the average wage (this is how Eurostat defines this series).

Belgium, 2024

Difficult words explained

GDP
Gross domestic product: the value of everything a country produces in a year. It is the usual measure of the size of an economy.
As a percentage of GDP
An amount compared with the size of the economy. This lets you compare countries of different sizes fairly.
Provisional
A first figure that the source may still revise. We always show the latest version and keep track of revisions.
