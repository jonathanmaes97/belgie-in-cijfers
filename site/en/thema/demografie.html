State of the Union / Population

How many of us are there, and who is joining us?

How many people live in Belgium and how old they are determines how many schools, homes, care services and pensions are needed. The population changes through births and deaths, and through people moving to or leaving Belgium. Here you can see both.

Who decides?

Population figures come from the National Register. Migration policy is federal; integration is the responsibility of the communities.

A government is never the only cause of how a figure develops. Many figures only respond to policy years later, and they also depend on the world economy, European rules and the policies of the regions and communities.

People aged 65 and over

How many inhabitants are 65 or older?

20.2%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is lower than the EU average (22.0%).
  • In 2015 it was 18.1%.

The larger this share, the more is needed for pensions and care, and the fewer people of working age there are to pay for it.

Net migration

How many people are added through migration, on balance?

6.4 per 1,000

Belgium, 2025 provisional

  • That is higher than the EU average (4.6 per 1,000).
  • In 2015 it was 5.5 per 1,000.

This covers everyone who moves, including Belgians who leave for abroad or come back. A positive figure means more people come to live here than leave.

Number of people moving to the country minus the number leaving, per 1,000 inhabitants (including statistical adjustment).

Natural increase

Are more people born than die?

−0.3 per 1,000

Belgium, 2025 provisional

  • That is higher than the EU average (−3.0 per 1,000).
  • In 2015 it was 1.0 per 1,000.

Together with net migration, this determines whether the population grows. A negative figure means more people die than are born.

Born abroad

How many inhabitants were born abroad?

20.2%

Belgium, 2025

  • That is higher than the EU average (14.4%).
  • In 2015 it was 15.9%.

The country of birth says nothing about nationality: Belgians born abroad are counted too, while children of migrants born here are not.

Share of inhabitants born outside the country, regardless of their nationality.

First-time asylum applications

How many people apply for asylum for the first time?

27,540

Belgium, 2025

  • In 2015 it was 39,065.

An asylum application is not yet a recognition: some applications are rejected. Only first applications count here, not repeated ones.
